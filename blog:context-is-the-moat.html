Context is the moat: why generic AI tools fail at marketing

July 14, 2026 Alex Duncan Founder, Vorsa

Open any AI writing tool right now and type: "Write a B2B email about our new payments product."

You'll get something technically competent. Grammatically correct. Probably formatted correctly. And completely indistinguishable from the hundred other B2B payment emails hitting your prospect's inbox this week.

This is the core problem with how most marketing teams are using AI today. They're treating it as a faster typewriter — and the output sounds like one.

The generic problem

Generic AI tools have no context about your brand. They don't know:

  • What specific problem you solve and for whom
  • The precise language your buyers use to describe their pain
  • The claims you're cleared to make vs. the ones you can't touch
  • The voice that earned you the customers you already have
  • The competitive context that shapes every positioning decision

Without that context, the model defaults to the statistical average of marketing copy it was trained on. And the statistical average of marketing copy is the same brand-agnostic filler that's been cluttering inboxes for a decade.

The problem isn't that AI can't write good marketing copy. It's that good marketing copy requires knowing things the model doesn't know.

Why the context layer is where differentiation lives

Think about what separates a great senior copywriter from a competent junior one. It's not vocabulary. It's not grammar. It's accumulated context — they know your customers, your category, your competition, your voice, and the three things you can never say in a sales call.

That accumulated context is exactly what's missing from generic AI tools. They can imitate structure and style. They cannot replicate knowledge.

The fix is not a better prompt. It's a persistent context layer that lives between your brand and the model — a repository that holds everything the model needs to generate output that actually sounds like you.

What that context layer actually contains

A real context repository isn't a brand voice doc. Those are written for humans. A repository built for AI needs to encode:

  • Company context — what you do, for whom, and the specific outcome customers get
  • Buyer profile — the exact person experiencing the pain, the words they use, what keeps them up
  • Positioning — concrete differentiators with numbers, not adjectives
  • Voice model — word-level and rhythm-level guidance, with examples your agent can study
  • Hard guardrails — words you never use, claims you can't make, regulatory context
  • Approved evidence — the specific studies, stats, and sources you're cleared to cite

When you build that repository once and feed it to the model on every generation call, the output stops sounding generic. It starts sounding like you — because the model is operating with the knowledge that makes you different.

The compounding advantage

Here's the part most teams miss: this context gets better over time.

Every campaign you build adds signal. Every asset that converts tells you something about what resonates. Every QA flag teaches you a new rule. The repository compounds — and the model's outputs get more differentiated with every iteration.

A generic AI tool has no memory of your last campaign. A context-first system has memory of all of them. That's the moat.

The teams that win the AI-in-marketing era won't be the ones who found the best model. They'll be the ones who built the richest context layer around a good one.
